## 0. Executive framing

The Bangladeshi teacher is not un-digital. She is _intensely_ digital — but her digital life is entirely inside Facebook groups, WhatsApp, and PDF files passed hand to hand. Every function Acadigma Campus proposes to sell her (job discovery, material sharing, material selling, peer reputation, lesson prep) **already happens at enormous scale**, just in a medium with no payments, no verification, no search, and no ownership.

The opportunity is not to create a behaviour. It is to give an existing behaviour a spine.

### 2.3 Plagiarism and attribution norms — the hard truth

**There is effectively no attribution norm.** A PDF entering a Facebook group loses its author within one re-share. Watermarks are stripped by re-screenshotting. The guide-book publishing industry itself is built on repackaging.

Compounding this, Bangladesh has a documented history of exam-material integrity failure: [question paper leaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Question_paper_leaks_in_Bangladesh) affected **all Bangladesh public examinations in 2014**. Teachers are therefore _culturally primed_ to treat exam-adjacent digital material as free-floating.

> **Implication for F-CM-03/F-CM-04:** watermarked signed downloads (already in PRD) are necessary but will not stop leakage. The defensible moat is **not DRM — it is the seller's verified identity and review history**. Buyers must be buying _"a vetted teacher's current-syllabus pack with a support channel"_, not _"a PDF"_. Price the relationship, not the file.

### 2.4 Realistic price points **[EST]**

Anchored to observed BD willingness-to-pay (a month of tuition = ৳3,500–6,000; a printed guide book ≈ ৳150–400):

| Product                                           | Suggested price | Rationale                        |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Single worksheet / topic pack                     | ৳30–80          | Below a photocopy run            |
| Chapter question bank                             | ৳100–250        | At guide-book parity             |
| Full-subject term suggestion + marking scheme     | ৳300–600        | ~10% of a month's tuition        |
| Year-long section bundle (one subject, one grade) | ৳1,000–2,000    | School-funded purchase (F-CM-04) |

At 30% commission and the PRD's **৳1,000 minimum payout** (F-CM-05), a seller must clear ~৳1,430 gross to cash out. **See §8 risk.**

> **Cross-check that matters:** a mid-tier English-medium teacher on ~৳30,000/month earns ~৳1,000/working-day. Any Acadigma feature that costs her more than ~15 minutes/day must return more than that in value, or it will be abandoned. This is the real performance budget behind the PRD's "≤60 second attendance" goal (§3.1).

### 4.5 Hiring pain observed in the wild

From the BEMSTA group feed, 2026-09-17 — anonymous posts asking about a named school's work environment, with replies alleging low pay and inexperienced hiring, and _"Did you attend the interview today?"_. Candidates are crowd-sourcing employer due diligence **anonymously**, because attaching their name is career-risky.

> **Design consequence for F-OP-01:** candidate-side reputation must be **asymmetric**. A candidate should be able to see verified information about a school without the school seeing that she looked. "Open to work" (already in PRD) must be **private-by-default with selective disclosure**, or it becomes a resignation announcement.

## 5. AI usage among Bangladeshi teachers

### 5.1 Adoption is real, and free-tier-bound

The best available BD dataset (universities, but the closest proxy) — n=381 teachers, n=384 students, plus 32 interviews; [PMC12461191](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12461191/), published 2025-03-06, rev. 2025-09-05:

| Finding                                                         | Figure       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| Teachers using **free** ChatGPT                                 | **79.3%**    |
| Teachers using **paid** ChatGPT                                 | **20.7%**    |
| Students using free                                             | **97.1%**    |
| Students using paid                                             | 2.9%         |
| Teacher use: verifying information accuracy                     | M = 3.88     |
| Teacher use: seeking training opportunities                     | M = 3.81     |
| Teacher use: research activities                                | M = 3.74     |
| **Teacher use: developing teaching materials and lesson plans** | **M = 3.73** |

### 5.2 School-level evidence

[TBS, 2025-08-30](https://www.tbsnews.net/features/panorama/ai-classrooms-how-bangladeshi-schools-are-adapting-new-digital-era): teachers at Mastermind School and International School Dhaka report using AI for **lesson planning, content creation, syllabus updating, personalised instruction, question-paper structuring and assignment design.** A Cambridge examiner/senior English instructor: _"It is not just the students who use AI. Teachers and schools are using it too."_ Adroit International School has engaged Pearson on curriculum integration; some teachers hold "AI Essentials for Educators" certification.

[ELT/EFL study, Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-025-13515-7) (abstract via search; full text paywalled): Bangladeshi ELT teachers use ChatGPT for **generating practice tasks, preparing quiz/exam question material, and automated feedback**; cite **time-saving, unlimited resources, easy accessibility**; worry about **student over-dependence and plagiarism**.

### 5.3 The three constraints

| Constraint                                                                                                | Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cost** — premium subscription is prohibitive for most                                                   | PMC12461191: "Financial constraints: Premium subscription costs prohibit most users." A $20/mo ChatGPT Plus ≈ **৳2,400/mo** — **8–30% of a private-school teacher's monthly salary** **[EST]** |
| **Connectivity** — needs robust high-speed networks                                                       | PMC12461191                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Bangla quality** — _"Its answers in English are always better"_; Bengali responses often contain errors | PMC12461191 (teacher quote)                                                                                                                                                                    |

Academic corroboration on Bangla quality: LLMs show _"inferior performance … for different Bangla NLP tasks"_ ([BenLLMEval, arXiv:2309.13173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13173)); _"consistent performance gaps for Bengali compared to English, particularly for smaller models"_ ([arXiv:2507.23248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23248)). Dedicated models (BanglaBERT, TituLLM, [TigerLLM](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-short.69.pdf)) exist but are research-grade.

### 5.4 Trust posture

Teachers are **more cautious than students** (PMC12461191). Representative teacher quote: _"ChatGPT is great for basic understanding, but it provides wrong information which can cause disastrous effects."_ Concerns: inaccurate citations, fabricated sources, over-reliance, and **lack of institutional guidance / ethical use policy.**

> **Product consequence for F-TE-01/F-TE-03:** the winning AI positioning in Bangladesh is _not_ "AI does it for you." It is **"AI drafts, you approve, the school has a policy."** Every AI output must be editable, attributable to the approving teacher, and logged. The PRD already does this for report-card comments ("stored AI comments with teacher approval", §5.5) — **that pattern should be the universal default across all AI tools, not a report-card special case.** And the credit ledger (F-TE-02) is not merely a billing mechanism: **for a teacher who cannot afford ৳2,400/month, school-funded AI credits are the entire value proposition.**

4. **44.5% internet penetration vs 93% private schooling** means a meaningful minority of _parents_ are unreachable. The parent portal must degrade to SMS.

## 8. Teacher adoption model

### Stage 0 → 1: What triggers **first use**

Ranked by observed pull strength in the research.

| #   | Trigger                                                                                                                                                                                             | Evidence base                         |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **A job she wants is posted only on Acadigma.** Job-seeking is the single most intense teacher behaviour online (3.7M memberships in NTRCA groups, 172.8K in Vacancy Hub, 90+ posts/day sustained). | §1.1, §4                              |
| 2   | **Her school's principal makes attendance mandatory.** Top-down, 100% conversion, zero desire.                                                                                                      | PRD §3.4                              |
| 3   | **A tutor wants to stop paying a 40–50% media fee** and needs her own student list.                                                                                                                 | §3.3                                  |
| 4   | **Free AI credits** — she is on the free ChatGPT tier (79.3%) and cannot afford ৳2,400/mo.                                                                                                          | §5.1, §5.3                            |
| 5   | **A peer she trusts sells something and shows the earnings screenshot.**                                                                                                                            | §1.1 certificate-screenshot behaviour |

> **The decisive insight:** triggers 1 and 3 are _pre-school_ — they reach the teacher **before** her employer adopts anything. The school-sales motion is slow and top-down; the teacher-acquisition motion via hiring and tutoring is fast and bottom-up. **Acadigma should acquire teachers first and sell schools second.** The hiring module (F-OP-01) and personal workspace (F-ID-02) are therefore **not R3/R0 afterthoughts — they are the customer-acquisition engine**, and their current placement in the release map (hiring in R3) is the largest strategic risk in the plan. See §10.

### Stage 1 → 2: What makes it **daily**

A tool becomes daily only when it sits on the critical path of something she must do anyway.

| Daily hook                   | Feature            | Why it sticks                                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Attendance                   | F-AC-03            | Legally/administratively mandatory. **≤60s or she reverts to paper.** |
| Her own timetable            | F-AC-06            | Answers "where am I now" — the most-repeated daily question           |
| Messages from office/parents | F-OP-06            | Fear of missing something                                             |
| Lesson log                   | F-TE-01            | Only if it takes <30s and feeds pacing automatically                  |
| Tutoring session log         | Personal workspace | Because it is _payment evidence_ (§3.3)                               |

**Anti-patterns that will kill daily use:**

- Any flow requiring a desktop mid-day.
- Re-login on a phone that swaps between 2G/4G.
- A lesson planner that is a blank text box. It must open with AI-drafted content she edits — teachers with no formal training (§1.3) do not have a lesson-plan template in their heads.
- Notification volume that trains her to swipe away.

### Stage 2 → 3: What makes her **invite others**

Teachers evangelise **status and money**, not software.

| Evangelism driver                         | Mechanism                                                                             | Evidence                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A verifiable credential artefact**      | Verified-teacher badge, shareable profile URL, instantly-issued                       | Teachers post certificate screenshots and complain about 12-hour delays (§1.1, শিক্ষক বাতায়ন) |
| **An earnings screenshot**                | Seller earnings page designed to be screenshotted — clean, ৳ figure prominent, no PII | TPT seller-blog culture (§6.1); BD screenshot-sharing norm                                     |
| **Getting a job through it**              | "I got hired via Acadigma" is the highest-status post possible in a 999K-member group | §1.1, §4                                                                                       |
| **Saving a colleague from an agency fee** | Referral within tuition-media groups                                                  | "Free Tution Media Sylhet (without media fee)" group name (§1.2)                               |
| **Anonymous employer review**             | A safe version of what BEMSTA does unsafely                                           | §4.5                                                                                           |

**Design mandate:** every one of these must produce a **single, beautiful, Bangla-capable, 1080×1080 shareable image**, generated server-side, with no personal data beyond what the teacher chose. Facebook is the distribution channel; if the artefact isn't screenshot-native, there is no loop.

## 9. "First 50 sellers" recruitment plan

**Constraint: this list is page/group-level only. No private individuals are named, and none should be scraped. Recruitment must be by public post or by the group's own admin channel, with admin permission.**

### The target mix

Per the TPT power law (§6.3), do not recruit 50 identical people. Target:

| Cohort                                                                                                               | Count | Why them                                                                                                                                         | Approach                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. English-medium subject leads** (BEMSTA 43.2K, ETAB 11K, English Medium School Jobs & Teachers' Community 32.7K) | 12    | Already produce worksheets in English; comfortable with digital files; highest price tolerance; Edexcel/Cambridge material is scarce and premium | Post in-group _with admin permission_ offering a **paid commission (৳3,000–5,000/pack)** for the first cohort — Twinkl model, not TPT model (§6.2). Do not lead with "earn royalties." |
| **B. Coaching-centre / suggestion authors** (SSC/HSC suggestion groups: 604K, 283K, 110K, 60K…)                      | 8     | Already monetise. Already have a buyer relationship. Already understand pricing.                                                                 | Approach as a **distribution upgrade**: "you already sell; we handle payment, delivery and piracy watermarking, you keep 70%." Highest immediate GMV.                                  |
| **C. Top tutors from tuition-media groups** (Dhaka Tuition Media 163K, Trust 56K, Best teachers media 87K)           | 10    | Have proprietary material built for individual students; motivated by the 40–50% fee grievance                                                   | Lead with the **free personal workspace**, convert to seller in month 2 once they have material in the library. **Workspace-first, marketplace-second.**                               |
| **D. Teacher-trainers** (Trainer & Assessor Forum 32K, Teacher Training Group 12K, BTAF, BSTC 1.4K)                  | 6     | Produce training decks and rubrics; carry authority; their endorsement converts others                                                           | Offer **co-branded listings** + a visible "Trainer" badge tier.                                                                                                                        |
| **E. শিক্ষক বাতায়ন / govt-portal actives** (299.5K)                                                                 | 6     | Massive reach; already upload content to a government portal for free and for status                                                             | Lead with **instant certificate/badge issuance** — the exact thing the government portal fails at (§1.1).                                                                              |
| **F. Special-needs & niche subjects** (BSTC 1.4K; madrasah; ICT)                                                     | 5     | Zero competing supply; small but fiercely loyal buyer base; proves catalogue breadth                                                             | Direct outreach via group admins; commission every pack.                                                                                                                               |
| **G. Schools as institutional sellers** (pilot schools' own question banks)                                          | 3     | One school = dozens of ready assets; solves cold-start catalogue depth                                                                           | Bundle into the school contract: "your archive, listed, revenue shared."                                                                                                               |

**Total: 50.**

### Sequencing

1. **Weeks 1–4 — Commission, don't recruit.** Pay cohorts A, D and F a flat fee for ~40 packs. Catalogue must be non-empty before any seller is asked to take revenue risk.
2. **Weeks 5–8 — Migrate cohort B.** They bring buyers with them.
3. **Weeks 9–16 — Convert cohort C** from free workspace users who already uploaded materials.
4. **Throughout — instrument cohort E** as the referral amplifier.

### Approach notes (non-negotiable)

- **Always go through group admins first.** These groups are heavily moderated and self-promotion is removal-worthy. A group admin who becomes a paid seller is worth 50 cold posts.
- **Never DM scraped members.** Reputationally fatal in a market this interconnected, and it is exactly the behaviour that makes teachers distrust "tuition media."
- **Lead with ৳, in ৳, with a number.** "Earn up to X" is noise; "we will pay you ৳4,000 for this pack" is a job offer.
- **Bangla-first collateral** for cohorts B, C, E; **English-first** for A and D.
- **Show the payout, not the platform.** The first screenshot that circulates determines whether cohort 2 exists.
