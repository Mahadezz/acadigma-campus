## 1. Bangladesh market

### 1.1 What the market actually looks like

The BD school-software market is a **fragmented, agency-led, on-premise-leaning ERP market**, not a SaaS market. The dominant shape is:

- A local software house (10–60 people) sells a web ERP to schools it can reach by phone and field visit.
- Pricing is **per-student per-month plus a one-time setup fee**, quoted privately, not published.
- The feature list is near-identical across every vendor: admission, student info, attendance (with biometric/RFID), fees & accounts, exams & results, SMS to parents, payroll, library, transport, inventory, a parent portal and a thin Android app.
- **Fees collection and accounting is the anchor module.** It appears first or second on every vendor's list. Attendance and results are secondary. This is the single most important structural fact in this document.
- Deployment is cloud-branded but frequently a single-tenant install per school ("installation within 15 working days" is a common promise — that is an install, not a signup).

The largest self-reported player found is **Eduman** (by Leadswin), claiming **5,000+ institutions / 3,000,000+ students / 40+ districts**, offering fees collection, SMS, results and a live **bKash merchant** integration. It was missing from this document entirely in the prior version despite that scale; flagged and profiled here (§1.2). **School360** (Spate Initiative Ltd) is the next largest self-reported player, claiming 996+ schools / 470,800+ students / 21,030+ teachers / 105,080+ parents, and BASIS National ICT Award 2020 champion. (Sources: Leadswin/Eduman vendor pages; school360.com.bd — both vendor self-reported, unaudited. _"Largest" here means largest by self-reported scale, not independently verified — the prior wording ("largest verified player by self-reported scale") self-cancelled and is corrected. (amended per SYNTHESIS)_)

### 1.3 Government and telecom-adjacent

- **Shikkhok Batayon / Teachers' Portal** (a2i, ICT Division + Cabinet Division, UNDP-supported; built with Riseup Labs). A **teacher professional-development and content-sharing portal**, not a school ERP. 650,000+ registered educators; the programme targets ~900,000 teachers across the Education and Primary & Mass Education ministries. Won the **UN WSIS Award 2024** (capacity building).
  - **Why this matters to Acadigma:** it is the closest thing in Bangladesh to a _national teacher-content network_ — and it is **free and non-commercial**. It is not a competitor for school operations, but it is the reference point teachers will compare a paid marketplace against. It also proves the behaviour (teachers uploading and sharing materials nationally) exists at scale in Bangladesh.
- **a2i / ICT Division** — no evidence found of a government school-ERP product competing with commercial vendors. Government activity is portals, content and teacher training. This includes **IEIMS** (SHED/BANBEIS — Student Unique ID, EIIN logins, no fees) and **EMIS** (DSHE) _(added per SYNTHESIS)_: both are free government reporting/retention-service paperwork schools must file, not competing school-management ERPs — and incumbent vendors' field agents commonly file this paperwork on the school's behalf as part of their service relationship, which is itself a retention lever worth noting, not evidence of government competition.
- **Telecom-backed:** no telecom operator sells a school ERP. Both operators are in **consumer edtech content**: Grameenphone bundles **Shikho** courses via MyGP at discounted rates (targeting Classes 6–10 / SSC) and runs **Grameenphone Academy**; **Robi–10 Minute School** is a free JSC/SSC/HSC study platform. These compete for parent/student attention and for the "edtech" label — **not** for the school's operating budget.
- **bKash "Education Fee"** — a first-class product line in the bKash app, live for **~1,000 educational institutions**, with instant digital receipts, USSD (`*247#`) support for non-smartphone users, and a redesigned, more user-friendly Education Fee icon. Cantonment institutes' fees are enabled **free of charge**.
  - **Why this matters:** bKash has already trained Bangladeshi parents that _school fees are paid inside bKash, by Student ID_. Any fee module Acadigma builds must terminate in bKash/Nagad, not a card page. Third parties (EPS, FEMS, CloudCampus24, Dhaka Bank) sell exactly this ERP↔bKash reconciliation bridge — i.e. **there is a paid market for the fee-collection layer alone**.

### 1.4 What schools complain about — honest status

**I could not verify complaints.** Play Store review text was not retrievable through the available tooling, no BD software review site exists, and Facebook groups are not indexable. The only concrete complaint evidence found was for **Banglar Shiksha** (an Indian West Bengal government app, not a BD product): "not working or showing wrong information again and again", with unaddressed complaints — not transferable.

**Inferred pain points (all unverified, all worth interviewing for):**

1. **Per-school installs mean per-school bugs.** "Installation within 15 working days" + "24/7 WhatsApp support with screenshot submission" (vendors' own copy) describes a support model built around things going wrong in a bespoke deployment.
2. **SMS cost is a live grievance.** Masking SMS runs **৳0.25–0.52 each**. A 600-student school sending two parent messages a week spends roughly ৳2,500–5,000/month on SMS alone — comparable to the entire ERP subscription. Schools will have opinions about this.
3. **The teacher is not the customer.** Every BD product is sold to the office. Teacher-facing surfaces are data-entry screens. This is the wedge Acadigma is built on, and the one thing no incumbent's architecture makes easy to fix.
4. **Mobile app as a paid add-on** (schoolsoftware-bd explicitly lists "mobile app licensing" as a cost line) means many schools are running ERP without a working parent app.

## 2. Global and regional references

### 2.1 What a Bangladeshi school would actually recognise

Ranked by likely recognition in a Dhaka English-medium staffroom:

1. **Google Classroom** — near-universal. Free, entrenched since COVID, and the real incumbent for anything assignment-shaped. Research on BD/South Asian classrooms documents Google Classroom + WhatsApp as the de-facto pair, with one study finding 79.1% of students rated Google Classroom effective vs WhatsApp.
2. **WhatsApp** — the actual parent-communication system of record in Bangladesh. Not a product to beat on features; a habit to absorb. (Acadigma's `contact_log` decision — logging WhatsApp/phone hand-offs — is the right instinct and should be promoted, not hidden.)
3. **Cambridge / Pearson Edexcel portals** — every O/A-Level school runs on these for entries and results. Not competitors, but an **integration expectation**.
4. **PowerSchool, Fedena, ClassDojo, Toddle** — recognised by international-curriculum schools and by the handful of schools that have looked abroad. Not recognised by the mid-market Bangla-medium or budget English-medium school.
5. **OpenSIS, Gibbon, Frappe Education, Classter, eduWave, Skolera, Kinderpedia** — effectively unknown in BD. Relevant only as build references.

### 2.3 Table stakes vs differentiators (global view)

**Table stakes** — a school will not sign without these, and will not pay extra for them:
student records & enrolment · attendance with a register · timetable · exams, marks and report cards · **fee collection and a financial ledger** · parent portal · staff records · role-based access · notifications to parents (SMS in BD, push elsewhere) · bulk import · printable/PDF everything · a mobile app · bilingual UI (BD-specific).

**Differentiators** — what people actually switch for:
teacher time-to-task on a phone · **AI lesson/assessment generation that is metered and honest** · curriculum pacing tied to the timetable · an integrated marketplace (nobody does this) · hiring/ATS inside the SIS (nobody does this) · automated cover-teacher assignment (nobody does this) · explainable student-risk scoring · offline attendance · an audit trail as a sellable feature.

**Table stakes that are secretly differentiators in BD:**
a genuinely fast Android experience · Bengali PDFs that render correctly · multi-tenant SaaS with no per-school install · not charging separately for the mobile app.

**Read-across for Acadigma's 30% flat commission:** it sits **between TPT Basic (45%) and TPT Premium / Tes Gold (20%)**, and is _better than the entry tier of both majors_. That is defensible. But note what both majors actually do: **they make the take rate earn-downable** — 45%→20% at TPT, 40%→30%→20% at Tes. Acadigma's PRD rules out per-seller negotiation in v1 (decision 4.3), which is correct for launch simplicity; the _structure_ to lower it later must exist, and since `commission_bps` is already snapshotted per order line, it does.

### 3.2 Seller verification

- TPT's public help content does not document a seller identity-verification requirement; enforcement is **reactive** — DMCA notice-and-takedown under its Copyright & Trademark Policy, with content removed on a valid notice.
- Acadigma's model is the opposite: **proactive KYC (2-business-day SLA) plus pre-publication moderation of every listing.** That is a genuine differentiator and the right call for a small market where trust must be manufactured rather than assumed — but it is also a **fixed operational cost per listing** that TPT does not carry. At 100 listings (the 6-month goal) that is fine; at 10,000 it is a team.

### 3.3 Refunds and what keeps sellers

- **TPT refunds:** digital sales are treated as final and non-refundable, with case-by-case exceptions — damaged or incomplete file, material misrepresentation, an undisclosed technical requirement, duplicate purchase, or a file never accessed. When TPT removes a resource for IP infringement it often issues **proactive refunds to buyers from the preceding year**.
  - Acadigma's 7-day, reason-required, platform-staff-only refund window is **more generous than TPT**. Combined with the 7-day earnings hold, exposure is bounded. Good design.
- **What keeps sellers** (from the structure of both majors): (1) an earn-down take rate, (2) predictable payouts, (3) discoverability they cannot get elsewhere, (4) protection from theft, (5) a store they own. Acadigma has (2), (4) and (5) by design; it has (1) as a latent capability; it **does not yet have (3)** — and (3) is the only one that matters at zero scale. See §7a.

### 3.4 Bangladesh / Bangla-language material marketplaces

**None exist.** Searching for Bangla/Bangladeshi teacher-resource marketplaces returns only: Bangladesh-_topic_ resources sold on TPT and Tes by non-Bangladeshi sellers, university language-learning resource lists, and NGO free-resource hubs (e.g. ebtd.education). The only at-scale Bangla teacher-content network is **Shikkhok Batayon**, which is free and government-run.

**Interpretation — and this cuts both ways:**

- _Opportunity:_ a completely open category with no incumbent take rate to undercut.
- _Risk:_ the absence of any commercial Bangla teacher-material marketplace, in a country with ~900,000 teachers and an active free national sharing portal, is at least as consistent with **"teachers here share for free and will not pay"** as with "nobody has built it yet." A free, award-winning, government-backed alternative is the hardest possible thing to price against.
- This is the **single highest-risk assumption in the product** and the cheapest one to test: put 20 paid listings in front of 200 teachers before building the full commerce stack.

### 4.2 What a portable teacher profile needs to beat this

Bdjobs' weakness is precise and exploitable: **৳3,098–6,195 buys a school 30 days of visibility and a pile of unstructured CVs.** It sells reach; it does not reduce work. Acadigma's hiring module sells the opposite. To win, the portable profile needs:

1. **Verified, structured credentials** — subjects taught × grade bands × years, not a PDF. Bdjobs cannot do this because it is horizontal across every industry; Acadigma can because it is vertical.
2. **Platform-verified degree and identity** (already designed: a KYC-style queue in the same console as sellers). A "verified" badge on a teacher profile is worth more in BD than almost anywhere, because certificate fraud is the stated reason schools fall back on word-of-mouth.
3. **Evidence of practice pulled from the system of record** — years on Acadigma, sections and subjects taught, attendance-taking consistency, published marketplace listings, lesson plans authored. **This is the moat: a teacher's profile is backed by their actual work history in Campus**, and no job board can replicate it. It must be opt-in and teacher-controlled — the teacher chooses what to show.
4. **Free to the teacher, forever, and portable across schools** — including after they leave, and including teachers whose school does not use Acadigma (the personal workspace already provides this).
5. **`open_to_work` as a quiet signal, not a public broadcast.** Teachers cannot afford their employer seeing it. "Schools browse only opted-in profiles" is right, but it needs an explicit _hide from my current school_ control or teachers will never tick the box.
6. **Time-limited, revocable document consent** (already decided, §1.15) — a real trust differentiator over emailing your NID to a stranger.
7. **Zero-cost job posting at launch.** Do not try to beat ৳3,098 on price; beat it on **free**, and monetise via the Pro plan entitlement, which the PRD already does.

## 6. Regulatory and context

### 6.1 Boards and structure

- **General boards:** eleven education boards (Dhaka, Rajshahi, Chattogram, Khulna, Barishal, Sylhet, Comilla, Dinajpur, Mymensingh, plus Madrasah and Technical). Grading is **identical nationwide**.
- **EIIN** — Educational Institute Identification Number, the unique institution identifier issued by **BANBEIS** (Ministry of Education) for secondary and higher-secondary schools, NU-affiliated colleges, universities, technical institutes and **Alia madrasahs**. The PRD already stores it on `school_profiles`. Note: **English-medium and Qawmi institutions frequently do not have one** — EIIN must be optional, and it is.
- **English medium:** BANBEIS (Bangladesh Education Statistics 2022) counts **137 English-medium schools with 71,456 students**. The **Bangladesh English Medium Association disputes this**, claiming **300+ registered and unregistered schools with 300,000+ students**. Boards are predominantly **Cambridge International** and **Pearson Edexcel**, with Oxford AQA and IB also present.
  - **Market-sizing implication:** the addressable English-medium segment is **on the order of 150–350 schools**, not thousands. Ten schools in six months is ~3–6% of the segment — ambitious but achievable. It also means the segment **cannot alone support the business**, and the Bangla-medium / madrasa / coaching segments (where School360 and every other vendor live) matter sooner than the PRD implies.
- **Madrasa:** Bangladesh Madrasah Education Board (Alia, with EIIN) plus the Qawmi stream outside it. Pathshala and Hikmah explicitly sell to both. Acadigma's grade-level model (Play–Class 12) does not express Ebtedayee/Dakhil/Alim. Not needed for v1, but it is a named segment in every competitor's copy.

### 6.2 Curriculum — a significant finding

The **National Curriculum 2022/2023** — which removed exams in lower grades, merged streams and shifted to continuous classroom-based assessment — **has been substantially rolled back.**

- The Education Ministry concluded the new curriculum was **unfeasible to implement**, citing teacher preparation, unclear content and insufficient institutional capacity.
- The interim government **reinstated the Science / Humanities / Business Studies streams** at Class 9 and removed midterm evaluations.
- Students entering Class 10 in the 2025 academic year use textbooks based on the **revised National Curriculum 2012** for **SSC 2026**, with teaching methods and assessment realigned to NC-2012 — i.e. **back to traditional, exam-based assessment**.

**Product implication — this is unambiguously good news for Acadigma.** The entire exam/marks/GPA/report-card spine in PRD §5.2 is built for exactly the NC-2012 world Bangladesh has returned to. The BD GPA rules are confirmed correct and stable:

- A+ 80–100 = 5.00 · A 70–79 = 4.00 · A− 60–69 = 3.50 · B 50–59 = 3.00 · C 40–49 = 2.00 · D 33–39 = 1.00 · **F 0–32 = 0.00**; pass mark 33.
- **F in any compulsory subject ⇒ overall GPA 0.00 / Failed**, regardless of other grades. PRD decision 2.4 captures this correctly.
- **Optional (4th) subject rule — not in the PRD:** `GPA = [Σ compulsory grade points + (4th-subject point − 2.00)] ÷ number of compulsory subjects`, with zero bonus when the 4th-subject point ≤ 2.00, and **failing the 4th subject does not affect GPA**. This is a real, universal BD rule and the grade-scale engine must support it. **Flagged as a schema/spec gap.**

### 6.3 Data protection — the biggest new constraint

Bangladesh now has a real data-protection law, and it landed while this PRD was being written.

- **Personal Data Protection Ordinance 2025** issued by the President on **6 November 2025**; amended by ordinance on **5 February 2026**; **repealed and replaced by the Personal Data Protection Act, 2026 (Act 63 of 2026), passed by Parliament in April 2026.**
- **Scope:** controllers and processors in Bangladesh, plus **extraterritorial** reach over anyone targeting products or services at Bangladesh residents.
- **Consent:** must be "clear, explicit, specific, and freely given positive indication"; a higher "specific consent" standard applies to sensitive data.
- **Children:** defined as **under 18**. Processing requires **verifiable parental consent** from a guardian or legally empowered representative **until the child turns 18**.
- **Sensitive data** includes National ID, **biometric data**, genetic information and criminal records. Large-scale cross-border transfers of these face **government oversight**, with notification and disclosure of safeguards.
- **Regulator:** the **National Data Management Authority** — binding instructions, inspections, SOPs.
- **Breach notification** to the Authority where a breach causes significant damage, within the prescribed timeframe and manner.
- **Penalties:** administrative fines up to **৳25 lakh**, and up to **৳50 lakh** for designated significant data fiduciaries; additional fines for repeat violations.
- **Controller obligations:** appoint a **Chief Data Officer**, implement security measures, **retain records for 5 years**, publish privacy notices, honour access / rectification / erasure / **portability**, and bind processors by contract.

**What this means concretely for Acadigma — and none of it is in the PRD:**

1. **A whole product is now a compliance surface.** Campus is a controller (for its own users) and a **processor** (for each school's student data). It needs a **written data-processing agreement in the school onboarding flow**, not just a ToS.
2. **Verifiable parental consent** is required for processing children's data. Today the _school_ enters student data — probably defensible on the school's legal basis, but the **guardian invitation flow is the natural place to capture consent** and it currently captures none. Cheap to add now, expensive to retrofit.
3. **Biometrics on children are now a sensitive-data decision, not a feature decision.** Every BD competitor sells fingerprint/RFID/facial-recognition attendance. Acadigma's choice of **signed-token QR instead of biometrics** (decision 3.10) is, under PDPA 2026, a **compliance advantage worth marketing** — and a reason never to add fingerprint support. Do not treat it as a missing feature.
4. **Cross-border transfer is a live question.** Supabase and Vercel host outside Bangladesh, and AI prompts go to Anthropic outside Bangladesh. The PRD's rule that prompts carry **no student PII beyond first name + grade** is exactly the right mitigation and should be documented as a named compliance control, not just an engineering nicety. Region selection and a transfer-safeguards note need an owner decision.
5. **Localisation — imported from `COMPLIANCE-PDPA.md` §1.7, missing from this document until now** _(added per SYNTHESIS)_. Data classified **"confidential"** or **"restricted"** under the Act must be stored **within Bangladesh** — **[VERIFIED]**, two independent sources. Organisations using foreign cloud infrastructure must additionally keep **at least one synchronised real-time copy inside Bangladesh** — **[SINGLE-SOURCE]**. Bulk transfers of sensitive identifiers (fingerprints, DNA, NID numbers, passports) trigger **heightened scrutiny / advance regulatory approval** — **[VERIFIED]**. The government may order **cessation of a foreign cloud service within 60 days** on national-interest grounds — **[SINGLE-SOURCE]**. Acadigma Campus currently stores 100% of its data outside Bangladesh; if children's health records, NID scans or KYC documents fall into the "confidential"/"restricted" classes — the natural reading — the current architecture does not comply, and no contract clause fixes it. This is a bigger risk than the rest of this section previously reflected.
6. **Data portability and erasure** are statutory now, not goodwill. "Data export and deletion for schools on request" must become a **self-serve, logged capability with an SLA**.
7. **Chief Data Officer** — a named role the company must appoint.
8. **5-year record retention** interacts with "audit retention indefinite" (fine) and the 30-day account-deletion grace (fine), but needs a written retention schedule.

> **Recommendation:** open `docs/product/COMPLIANCE-PDPA.md` and treat PDPA 2026 as an R0/R1 requirement, not a pre-launch checkbox. The pentest requirement in PRD §6 is necessary but not sufficient.

### 6.4 Payments — hard numbers

- **SSLCommerz:** Basic plan setup fee **৳25,500** (one-time, non-refundable), including basic API integration and merchant dashboard access. **2.5%** per successful transaction on standard channels (Visa, Mastercard, bKash, Nagad, Rocket, internet banking), **3.5%** on American Express. Third-party reporting puts settlement at **2–5 business days** (**unverified**); another source quotes an older **৳15,000** setup figure (**superseded — the vendor page says ৳25,500**).
- **bKash merchant** reportedly **1.5%** (**unverified** — Facebook-sourced).
- **Bulk SMS:** **৳0.25–0.52 per masking SMS** depending on provider and volume; institutional masking sender IDs require a signed, sealed authorisation letter from the head of institution.

**Unit-economics implications:**

1. **৳25,500 is a hard cash cost before the first taka of revenue**, and the PRD assumes live keys arrive before R3. Budget it and start the merchant application early — onboarding requires company documents that take time in Bangladesh.
2. **2.5% MDR against a 30% commission** is ~8% of gross platform revenue on marketplace sales. Acceptable. But on a **৳100 listing** the platform keeps ৳30, pays ~৳2.50 MDR, then owes a payout transfer. The **৳1,000 minimum payout** is doing essential work here — keep it.
3. **Settlement lag of 2–5 days sits inside the 7-day earnings hold** — the hold is correctly sized.
4. **A school-fee module would put Acadigma in the flow of funds**, a materially different regulatory and treasury posture. Route to bKash/Nagad **as the school's merchant, not Acadigma's**, to avoid becoming a payment aggregator.

### 6.5 School fee practices

- English-medium monthly tuition spans roughly **৳6,000 to ৳80,000+**, with budget schools at **৳5,000–12,000/month** and mid-market commonly cited at **৳15,000–50,000/month**. Elite international schools run **৳10–15 lakh/year**.
- Admission fees range **৳20,000 to ৳500,000+**, with ~**৳80,000** cited as typical for a reputed Dhaka English-medium school. A government guideline capping metropolitan English-medium **admission + session fees at ৳30,000** has been reported — **this comes from an older Daily Star piece and its current force is unverified**; do not rely on it.
- The structure is **admission fee + annual session charge + monthly tuition + exam/activity/transport extras** — i.e. schools need an **arrears-aware ledger per student**, not a flat recurring charge.

**Affordability sanity check:** a 400-student school charging ৳10,000/month collects **~৳4,000,000/month**. The market ERP price for that school is **~৳2,800/month** — 0.07% of revenue. _(The claim "price is not the barrier in this segment" that previously appeared here is deleted — it is contradicted by other research in this document. Switching cost and trust are real barriers, but price cannot be waved away without evidence. amended per SYNTHESIS)_

### (a) Table-stakes features we are missing — ranked

1. **Student fee collection + per-student ledger + bKash/Nagad payment.** 🔴 **Ship-blocker for the mid-market.**

## 8. Positioning and pricing

### 8.1 Positioning statement options

**Option A — Teacher-first (recommended for teachers).**

> **Acadigma Campus is the school system your teachers actually open.** Attendance for a whole section in under a minute, on the phone already in their hand. Exams to Bengali report cards without a single Excel formula. Lesson plans drafted by AI and kept, not thrown away. And a marketplace where the worksheets they already make earn them money. Schools in Bangladesh have bought software for the office for fifteen years. This is the first one built for the staffroom.
>
> _Strength:_ attacks the one structural weakness every incumbent shares and only Acadigma's architecture can fix. _Risk:_ the buyer is the owner, not the teacher — needs an ROI line ("teachers who actually use it produce the data your reports were always missing").

**Option B — Owner / operations ROI (recommended for school sales).**

> **Everything your school does daily, in one place, for less than one student's monthly fee.** Attendance, timetable, exams, GPA report cards, parent portal, staff, hiring and cover teachers — one login, on any phone or the office PC, in Bangla and English. Every number on every dashboard comes from your own records, not a demo. And when a teacher is absent at 8:05, Acadigma has already worked out who covers which period.
>
> _Strength:_ speaks the buyer's language; the cover-teacher hook is concrete, daily and undemoed by anyone else. _Risk:_ "all-in-one ERP" is exactly how every incumbent describes itself — it invites a feature-checklist comparison Acadigma currently loses on fees, SMS and payroll.

**Option C — Category creation / network (recommended for investors and teacher recruitment).**

> **The first school platform in Bangladesh where teachers are the network.** Run your school on Acadigma Campus — and your teachers get a verified professional profile they keep for life, a place to sell the materials they make, and jobs that come to them. Schools get better teachers. Teachers get paid twice. Nobody else in this market is building the teacher side at all.
>
> _Strength:_ genuinely uncontested; explains the marketplace and hiring modules as strategy rather than scope creep. _Risk:_ it is a promise about a network that does not exist yet, sold to owners who are buying attendance and fee collection. **Do not lead with this in a school sales call.**

**Recommendation: A for teachers, B for owners, C for the story.** One product described to three audiences, not three strategies.

### 8.2 Pricing benchmark

**The BD market norm is per-student-per-month plus a one-time setup fee.** The only published BD price sheet found (Smart Software Ltd) is a clean volume-banded ladder:

| School size    | BD market rate (Smart Software Ltd) | Monthly total    | Setup   |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------- |
| 150 students   | ৳10/student                         | **৳1,500**       | ৳10,000 |
| 300 students   | ৳8/student                          | **৳2,400**       | ৳10,000 |
| 500 students   | ৳7/student                          | **৳3,500**       | ৳10,000 |
| 800 students   | ৳5–6/student                        | **৳4,000–4,800** | ৳10,000 |
| 2,000 students | ৳5/student                          | **৳10,000**      | ৳10,000 |

Cross-checks: Edufy's own blog puts the BD market at **৳10,000–50,000/year** (≈ ৳830–4,200/month), consistent with this ladder up to ~800 students. India's SchoolKnot is **₹10/student/month ≈ ৳14**, i.e. **Bangladesh is cheaper than India** on this metric.

**Acadigma's own pricing.** The plan-price matrix and student-band ladder that previously appeared here is superseded — **see `MARKET-STRATEGY.md` §c for the canonical price list** rather than restating numbers here. _(amended per SYNTHESIS)_ The qualitative finding stands, and is why the canonical grid is banded by student count rather than flat per plan: flat pricing with generous caps systematically overcharges small schools and undercharges large ones relative to the BD per-student market norm above, and the small-to-mid English-medium school (150–600 students) is precisely the stated target.

**Recommendation — remaining changes** (the plan-price ladder itself is now defined in `MARKET-STRATEGY.md` §c, not here):

1. **Add a one-time onboarding fee of ৳10,000–15,000.** Schools _expect_ it (every BD vendor charges ৳10,000+), it does not depress the recurring price, and it funds the single biggest adoption barrier: **migrating the school's existing student, fee and mark data**. Waive it for the first 10 schools in exchange for a case study. It also partly offsets the ৳25,500 SSLCommerz setup.
2. **Do not raise prices.** At 0.07–0.08% of a school's fee revenue the ERP line is not where the deal is won. Competing on price against ৳5/student vendors is a losing game; competing on teacher experience and the marketplace is not.
3. **Price SMS as metered pass-through** (buy at ৳0.25–0.52, resell in packs), never as a plan inclusion. Otherwise one chatty school destroys the margin on a ৳2,999 subscription.

**On the marketplace 30% commission:** in range and defensible — better than TPT Basic (45%) and Tes Bronze (40%). Keep it flat for v1. Put the **earn-down ladder** (30% → 20% above a BDT sales threshold in trailing 12 months, TPT/Tes style) on the roadmap, because it is the mechanism both majors use to retain their best sellers, and `commission_bps` is already snapshotted per order line so the data model is ready.

**On AI credit top-ups (৳499 / 500 credits):** no BD or Indian comparator exists, so this is unanchored. Sanity-check it against the actual Anthropic cost per action rather than against the market, and keep the pack small enough that a teacher can expense it personally — that is its real job.

## 9. What this research could not establish

Stated plainly, because these gaps should drive the next piece of work:

1. **No BD Play Store ratings or review text** — the tooling could not retrieve them. Competitor app quality is unmeasured.
2. **No Facebook group evidence** — not indexable. The complaint pain points in §1.4 are inference, not data.
3. **Published pricing for exactly one BD vendor.** Every other BDT figure is a range from a vendor blog or a marketplace snippet.
4. **No school interviews.** Nothing here reflects a Bangladeshi school owner's, admin's or teacher's actual words.
5. **The most important open question is unanswered: will Bangladeshi teachers pay for teaching materials?** There is no marketplace to observe, and a free government alternative (Shikkhok Batayon, 650,000+ teachers) exists. §3.4 argues both ways honestly. **Test with a 20-listing / 200-teacher pilot before building the full commerce stack in R3.**
6. **Teachmint's ERP exit is unconfirmed** and, if true, is the most important competitive datapoint in this document. Verify it properly.
7. **Trademark searches** (DPDT Bangladesh, WIPO) were not performed, for "Acadigma" or "TeachFlow".
8. **Cambridge / Edexcel integration expectations** were not researched in depth and matter for the English-medium segment.
9. **Madrasa grade structures** (Ebtedayee / Dakhil / Alim) were not modelled against `grade_levels`.
